    An independent personal project, not an official UN or ESCAP product, and not a warning system. For alerts, follow your national meteorological service.

    Method and sources

    Heat hazard. Absolute: the daily maximum apparent temperature (Open-Meteo), scaled from 24 °C (0) to 46 °C (1). Against local normal: the forecast daily maximum temperature minus the 1991–2020 monthly mean of daily maximum temperature (NASA POWER), full weight at +10 °C and counted only once apparent temperature passes 20 °C (full weight at 30 °C). Blend averages the two, so places are judged partly against what is normal for them. Category names follow the National Weather Service heat-index bands, but apparent temperature is not identical to the heat index.

    Vulnerability. A weighted average of percentile ranks: share aged 65+ and urban share (World Bank, national), city population (GeoNames), and built-up share minus tree-cover share within 5 km of the centre (ESA WorldCover 2021). You set the weights.

    Risk index. 100 × hazard × (0.4 + 0.6 × vulnerability).

    Neighbourhood grid. Selecting a city draws 64 cells of about 1.25 km showing built-up against tree cover. It is a land-cover proxy for heat retention and a 2021 snapshot, not measured surface temperature. Cities without land-cover data show no grid.

    AI briefing. Written at build time by Llama 3.3 70B on Groq, using only the figures for the ten hottest cities per region over three days. It can contain errors, so check it against the map.

    Limits. Age and urbanisation are national figures. There is no data on outdoor workers or cooling access. Monthly normals ignore change within the month. Forecasts are model output. Each country shows its largest cities plus its capital, from places above 15,000 people.
